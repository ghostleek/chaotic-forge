Summary

### Action Items

- [ ]  Decide which concept to submit: the open-world verbalization flow or the competitive mini-game assembly concept
- [ ]  Confirm the initial entry experience for the forge ("what do you see when you first enter") and whether it is "radically fun enough"

### Context & Time Pressure

- The team is under a tight deadline, with approximately 4.5 minutes/hours left for submission
- The project is referred to as "Fauna House" or "the Forge" - a platform where the game-building experience is itself a game

### Core Concept: Verbalize-to-Play Flow

- A user enters the forge, describes a game they want to play verbally, and predefined game design concepts surface as options based on what they articulate
- This is not fully unbounded - structured prompts guide both experienced and inexperienced users
- Open question: should the default view be an "open world card" with no options, or surface options immediately?
- 2D vs.3D preference was flagged as a potentially polarizing choice for gamers that the UI must address

### New Idea: Competitive Mini-Game Assembly Mechanic

- An alternative concept was proposed: the game-building experience is structured like a party game (compared to Wii party)
- Players compete in a series of mini-games; winners earn game mechanics or components (e.g. "control gravity") as rewards
- After completing several mini-games, each player assembles their own game from the components they've collected
- Players then compete to see whose assembled game is better
- This was compared to a game called "new bats" in terms of mechanics
- Concern raised that this relies heavily on a large agency API, making it potentially very complex to implement

### Open Questions & Next Steps

- The team is still deciding between the two directions: the original verbalize-to-play flow vs. the new competitive assembly mechanic
- If the new concept feels too unbounded, the group may revert to the earlier-discussed structured approach
- The "cards and game decomposition" guidance layer was noted as applicable to both concepts regardless of direction chosen
