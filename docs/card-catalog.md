# Forge card catalog and contribution rules

**Updated:** 13 September 2026  
**Status:** Product vocabulary and proposed card treatments; no claim that these cards or arbitrary combinations are implemented.  
**Decision source:** The user's correction to the initial-hand design. This document supersedes the earlier proposal that assigned FPS, Zombies and Cooking to different people.

## One open deck

Anyone can suggest any dimension. Cards can express game modes, mechanics, concepts, presentation, objectives, social rules or a familiar game reference. A card may carry several descriptive tags. **Tags describe an idea; they do not allocate a contribution category to a player.** There is no FPS seat, Cooking seat, mandatory dimension quota or requirement to fill one card from every category.

Everyone contributes to the initial creation. In subsequent rounds, the existing winner-and-loser rule still determines who can commit the next two additions: both get one addition each, and either can choose any kind of card. This freedom of subject matter does not silently grant middle-ranked players an extra committed addition.

Keep these layers visible:

1. **Source concept:** what the player actually chose, including its original card name and contributor.
2. **Forge interpretation:** the specific rule or behavior proposed for this creation, including how it interacts with other contributions.
3. **User decision:** the accepted contribution or an explicit revision. Preserve both the source and accepted interpretation in the saved history.

Selecting a broad reference such as Mario does not mean importing the entire commercial game. The eventual manifest must state the concrete behavior, controls and scoring implications. The effect examples below are proposals for interpretation, not preaccepted rules or proof of executable support.

Bridge is specifically the card game, not a bridge-building mechanic. Its usual partnership structure is a feasibility question for the proposed three-player demo, not permission to claim an unimplemented adaptation. Similarly, choosing Prompt & Punchline does not automatically replace the party loop's agreed performance ranking with a subjective vote.

## Draw, inspect, discard and replace

**Confirmed:** players may discard a card they do not understand and receive a new one. Make the action visible on each eligible uncommitted card, with plain wording such as **“Swap for a new card.”** A short explanation and inspect view can help, but neither should be a prerequisite for replacing an unfamiliar card.

**Proposed interface example:** display four playable concept cards plus one illustrative blank card in an initial hand. This count exists to make the layout concrete; it is not a confirmed deal size, hand limit or matching rule. Every position can contain any type of concept. There is no category-specific replacement slot.

Proposed replacement behavior for review:

- Replacing a hand card changes the options available to that player; it does not commit a contribution, spend their later-round edit or modify the active game.
- Remove the chosen card only when the replacement is confirmed. During a request, show that this one card is being exchanged; failures leave the old hand recoverable.
- Prefer a different card from the available eligible pool. If the pool is exhausted, explain that honestly; do not animate an invented successful draw. The accepted hand/replacement contract must settle reshuffling and duplicate policy.
- Do not attach a currency, payment, rarity advantage or reroll charge. No such economy was requested. Any future replacement limit or timing rule is a separate product decision.
- Once a contribution has been committed, revising it follows the room's explicit revision rules. Replacing a card in a hand is not a way to remove an already active mechanic.

Exact hand size, draw distribution, duplication, visibility to others and replacement limits remain proposed or unresolved. These must not reintroduce assigned categories through the deck algorithm.

## The conceptual blank card

**Confirmed:** one card in the initial hand is blank so a player can supply their own instructions. The blank is conceptual and excluded from the playable demo, but should appear in the illustrative UI.

Use a distinct card titled **“Your own idea”**, a short instruction placeholder, and a persistent **“Concept only · unavailable in this demo”** label. The study may illustrate entry or show example text, but must not show it as accepted, submitted, forged or playable. Entering text in a mockup is not a live generation call.

In a future supported flow, free text has the same provenance and interpretation requirements as a catalog card. It can describe any dimension and is not confined to a genre, input or aesthetic field. Its availability in later hands is not decided by the initial-hand instruction.

## Resolving the mashup without narrowing the premise

The group chooses ideas first; Forge interprets their combination. Examples are illustrative, not a generic base-game picker or proof that every combination runs.

For **2D + 3D**, show the interaction explicitly. A proposed interpretation might use a 3D world with movement constrained to a 2D plane, or explicit transitions between a flat puzzle and a spatial arena. The first interpretation must explain what each contribution visibly changes. If the runtime cannot preserve both contributions, show the conflict and offer revision; do not quietly discard one, invent a working result or substitute an unrelated preset.

Apply the same standard to conflicts in controls, perspective, player count, scoring or objectives. A supported manifest records which interpretation was accepted and which behaviors implement it. An ambiguous pair is an opportunity for a visible creative proposal, not a reason to assign different categories to people.

Preset demonstrations may qualify a finite set of combinations from this open vocabulary. Label available, illustrative and unsupported choices truthfully. A preset's coverage is an implementation boundary, not the product's contribution taxonomy. Broader catalog inclusion never guarantees arbitrary executable combinations.

## Visual direction and evidence

Give each card one original visual idea that remains recognizable at hand size, a short working title and a readable effect summary. Tags can help inspection, but should not dominate the art or become seats, columns or mandatory contribution lanes. Keep selected, submitted, unavailable and illustrative states distinct through words and shape, not color alone.

GPT-generated card art is build-time artwork unless an actual runtime generation flow is implemented. Record the actual generator/model when available, prompt, output path and intended card use. Do not label art as produced with a particular model or “Flare” setting without tool confirmation. Art quality does not establish gameplay support.

This document reports user direction and proposed design interpretation. It contains no observed playtest results, live room evidence, generated playable builds or external research claims.
